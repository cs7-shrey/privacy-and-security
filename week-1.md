## 1. Strength of Weak Ties

This is probably the most interesting concept in Week 1.

Strong ties: Close friends, family, people you interact with regularly.

Weak ties: Acquaintances, distant colleagues, people you barely know.

Counterintuitively, weak ties often provide more novel information and job opportunities than strong ties.

Why? Your close friends generally know the same people and information you do. Weak ties connect you to otherwise separate social circles.

This is associated with sociologist Mark Granovetter's The Strength of Weak Ties.

MCQ: Which concept explains why acquaintances can be more useful for finding jobs? → Strength of Weak Ties.

## 3. The Five Vs of Social Media

Very important for MCQs.

|V|Meaning|Example|
|---|---|---|
|Volume|Amount of data|Millions of posts|
|Velocity|Speed of data generation|Thousands of tweets per second|
|Variety|Different data types|Text, video, images, location|
|Veracity|Trustworthiness/accuracy|Whether news is fake|
|Value|Utility extracted from data|Targeted advertisements|

The two most easily confused:

- Volume = how much
- Velocity = how fast

Veracity is about the credibility and reliability of information, not its popularity.

## 6. Shadow Profiles

This is explicitly highlighted as important in Lecture 1.1.

A shadow profile is information a platform maintains about somebody who doesn't even have an account on that platform.

For example, other users upload their contact lists. A platform can potentially associate telephone numbers, names and connections with a nonmember.

When that person eventually joins, the platform may already have inferred information about them.

MCQ trap: Shadow profiles are not the same as fake accounts.

- Fake account → An account misrepresenting its identity.
- Shadow profile → Information maintained about someone, potentially without their participation.

## Social Media in Crisis Management and Protests

The lecture emphasizes that social media can both help resolve crises and contribute to organizing harmful activity.

| Event                             | Significance                                                                               |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Hudson River plane landing (2009) | Early landmark of Twitter in crisis response; Janis Krums posted a famous eyewitness photo |
| Lost child at railway station     | Viral social media post helped reconnect the child with parents                            |
| Nepal earthquake                  | Government and citizens coordinated information and relief                                 |
| Arab Spring                       | Social media helped mobilize protests                                                      |
